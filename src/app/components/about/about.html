Why MEAN-MultiChat Microservices

MEAN-MultiChat started as one Angular SPA talking to a single Express + MongoDB API — chat, images, RAG, YouTube, recipes, jokes, TTS, and accounts behind one /api surface. That was the right way to ship a learning workspace. The next step is to prove the same product as a strangler-fig microservices backend: keep the SPA unchanged, put a gateway in front, and peel each domain into its own Node process.

Today the browser still calls familiar paths (/api/chat, /api/rag, /api/youtube, …). An API gateway on port 4800 routes each prefix to a dedicated service — auth, rag, images, chat, media, speech, jokes, recipes, profile, usage, and platform — while Angular, Material, Observables, and JWT / Auth0 stay exactly as they were. Microservices here are a server-side morph, not a micro-frontend rewrite.

The impetus is still practical: learn by shipping. Evolving Multichat this way forces clear service boundaries, a shared JWT contract, and deployable peels you can smoke-test one tab at a time — a living portfolio of both product features and architecture.

Building with AI

AI is both a product surface here (chat, images, RAG) and a partner in how the app gets built. I use assistants to move faster on boilerplate, explore APIs, and polish UX — while keeping ownership of product decisions, security boundaries, and the final look and feel.

The goal isn't to replace craft; it's to amplify it — so more time goes into the experience you see in Chat, YouTube, Images, RAG, and the rest of the menu.
